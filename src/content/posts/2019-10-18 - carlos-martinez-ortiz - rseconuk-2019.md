The conference was really well organised, with a great keynote by [Andy Stanford-Clark](https://rseconuk2019.sched.com/speaker/andy_stanford_clark.1zns8cof), a very entertaining one by [Ben Goldcare](https://rseconuk2019.sched.com/speaker/invited_speaker.1zni68dt) and a very inspirational post-dinner talk by [Dr Maggie Aderin-Pocock](https://rseconuk2019.sched.com/speaker/dr_maggie_aderin_pocock.76qlrjy).

Last day of the conference was filled with a variety of exciting hands-on workshops at all levels of expertise, for example: learning how to set-up your own BinderHub instance, using Kubernetes in Google Cloud, building a sustainable team culture or becoming and RSE Superhero with VS Code and Azure Pipelines.

Here are some of the take-home messages which stuck in my mind:

* One recurring topic was that of career paths for RSEs. This was mentioned several times during the [UK-RSE Trustee flash presentations](https://rseconuk2019.sched.com/event/QSSO/society-of-research-software-engineering-agm), and it resonates with the same message being voiced during [de-RSE](/derse-2019-first-conference-for-research-software-engineers-in-germany-fa568e62ad00) and with similar discussions taking place in the Netherlands.
* It was mentioned several times that many people feel at home at this conference. It is very inspiring to see the strong feeling of community shared amongst participants.
* It was also mentioned that the [RSE community was created as a community of giving](https://twitter.com/chryswoods/status/1174014379945267201), where participants come to share their knowledge. Again, very inspiring to see such an open culture where helping others is given so much importance.
